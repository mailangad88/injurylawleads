Medical malpractice happens when a doctor, nurse, hospital or other healthcare provider fails to provide the level of care that a reasonably skilled provider would have provided in the same situation, and a patient is harmed as a result.

## The basic elements

1. **A provider-patient relationship** existed.
2. **The provider breached the standard of care**, meaning they did something a competent provider would not have done, or failed to do something they would have done.
3. **The breach caused an injury**, not just a bad outcome that would have happened anyway.
4. **The injury led to damages**, such as additional medical costs, lost income, pain or disability.

## Common types of malpractice

- **Misdiagnosis or delayed diagnosis**, such as missed cancer, stroke, heart attack or infection
- **Surgical errors**, including wrong-site surgery or objects left inside a patient
- **Medication errors**, such as the wrong drug or dose
- **Birth injuries** caused by mishandled labor and delivery
- **Anesthesia errors**
- **Failure to obtain informed consent** about significant risks

## Warning signs

You might want a lawyer to review what happened if:

- Your condition got significantly worse after treatment, and no one can explain why.
- A second doctor said your condition should have been caught earlier.
- You were told of a "complication" that seems like a mistake.
- You needed corrective surgery for something that went wrong.
- Your records are incomplete, or the provider has been reluctant to share them.

## Why these cases are different

Medical malpractice claims are among the most complex injury cases. Many states require expert support at an early stage, set special procedures, cap certain damages, and have deadlines that differ from other injury claims. Some deadlines run from when you discovered, or should have discovered, the injury, and some states have an outside limit regardless of discovery.

<Callout type="warning">

**Request your medical records.** You have a legal right to copies of your records. Getting them early helps a lawyer evaluate your case.
