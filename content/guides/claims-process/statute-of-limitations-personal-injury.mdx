Every state limits how long you have to file an injury lawsuit. That deadline is called the **statute of limitations**, and if it passes, you usually lose the right to recover anything, however strong your claim was.

<Callout type="warning">

Deadlines vary by state and by type of claim, and some are much shorter than people expect. This page explains how deadlines work in general. It is not a substitute for advice about your specific deadline.

</Callout>

## How the statute of limitations works

Each state's legislature sets deadlines for different kinds of lawsuits. For many personal injury claims, the period falls between one and six years, and two or three years is common. The deadline usually starts on the date of the injury.

Different deadlines may apply to the same accident. For example, the deadline for a property damage claim for your car may differ from the deadline for your injury claim, and a wrongful death claim may run from the date of death.

## Exceptions that can change your deadline

- **Claims against the government.** If a city bus, government employee, or poorly maintained public road was involved, you may need to file a formal notice of claim within months, long before the regular statute of limitations.
- **Medical malpractice.** Many states set special deadlines, sometimes running from when the injury was discovered, often with an outside limit.
- **Minors.** In many states, the deadline for an injured child is paused until they turn 18, but there are many exceptions.
- **Discovery rule.** Some deadlines start when you knew, or reasonably should have known, about the injury and its cause.
- **Defendant absence.** Some states pause the clock while the defendant is out of state.

## Why waiting is risky even before the deadline

Even when you have time left, waiting can hurt your case. Witnesses forget details, surveillance footage is deleted, vehicles are repaired, and insurers may argue that a delay in treatment means you were not seriously hurt. Lawyers also need time to investigate before filing.
