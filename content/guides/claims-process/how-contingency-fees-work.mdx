Many people who are hurt assume they cannot afford a lawyer. In personal injury cases that is rarely true, because most injury lawyers work on a **contingency fee** basis.

<ExplainerVideo slug="how-contingency-fees-work" />

## What "contingency" means

A contingency fee means the lawyer's fee is contingent on, or depends on, winning money for you through a settlement or verdict. If there is no recovery, there is generally no attorney fee. You pay nothing up front and nothing by the hour.

## How the fee is calculated

The fee is a percentage of the amount recovered. A common arrangement is around one third, sometimes increasing if the case goes into litigation or trial. The percentage, and whether it is calculated before or after case costs are deducted, should be spelled out in a written agreement. Some states cap fees for certain kinds of cases.

## Case costs vs. attorney fees

Separate from the fee, cases have costs: filing fees, medical record fees, expert witnesses, depositions and so on. Many firms advance these costs and are repaid from the recovery. Ask:

- Are costs deducted before or after the fee is calculated?
- If we lose, do I owe the costs?
- Will I get an itemized closing statement?

## Questions to ask in a free consultation

- What percentage do you charge, and does it change if we file a lawsuit or go to trial?
- Who will actually handle my case day to day?
- How often will I get updates?
- How long do cases like mine usually take?
- Have you handled cases like mine?
