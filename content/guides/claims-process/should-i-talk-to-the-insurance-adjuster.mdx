Soon after an accident, often within a day or two, you may get a call from an insurance adjuster. They may be friendly and sound eager to help. It helps to understand what their role is before you talk.

## What an adjuster does

An insurance adjuster investigates claims and decides how much the insurance company will pay. The adjuster for the at-fault party's insurer works for that company, and their job includes keeping payments as low as reasonably possible. That is not a criticism. It is how the system works.

## Things to be careful about

**Recorded statements.** The adjuster may ask to record your account. Your words can later be used to argue you were partly at fault or not seriously hurt. You can politely say you are not ready to give a recorded statement.

**Broad medical releases.** Signing a general authorization may let the insurer review your entire medical history, looking for pre-existing conditions to blame.

**Downplaying injuries.** When asked "How are you?", saying "I'm fine" is polite, but it can end up in the claim file. Simply say you are still being evaluated.

**Quick settlement offers.** An early check can be tempting, especially when bills are piling up. But accepting usually means signing a release that ends your claim permanently, even if you later need more treatment.

## What you can safely share

- Your name and contact information
- The date, time and location of the accident
- The names of other people involved
- Your insurance information

## When to bring in a lawyer

Consider talking to a lawyer before speaking further with the other party's insurer if you were hurt, if fault is disputed, or if the insurer is pressing you to settle quickly. Once you have a lawyer, the insurer must deal with them instead of you.
