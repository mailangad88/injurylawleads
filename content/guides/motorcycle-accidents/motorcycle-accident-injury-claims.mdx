Riders have almost no protection in a crash, so even a low-speed collision can cause fractures, road rash, spinal injuries or traumatic brain injury. Riders also face an assumption that other crash victims usually don't, which is that the rider must have been reckless.

## The bias against riders

Insurance adjusters, and sometimes jurors, may start from the belief that motorcyclists speed or weave through traffic. In reality, a common cause of motorcycle crashes is a driver who simply does not see the motorcycle, especially when turning left at intersections or changing lanes.

## Evidence that helps a motorcycle claim

- Photos of the scene, both vehicles and your gear
- Helmet camera or dashcam footage, if you have it. Save the original file.
- Witness names and contact information
- The police report
- Your damaged helmet and gear. Keep them; don't throw them away.
- Medical records documenting every injury

## Helmet laws and your claim

Helmet laws vary widely by state. Some require all riders to wear helmets, some only riders under a certain age, and some have no requirement. If you were not wearing a helmet, an insurer may argue your head injuries were worse as a result. That argument generally does not affect claims for other injuries, like a broken leg. How it plays out depends on your state's laws.

## Insurance sources

Besides the at-fault driver's liability coverage, check your own motorcycle policy for uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage and medical payments coverage. Riders are often hurt badly enough that the other driver's limits are not enough.
