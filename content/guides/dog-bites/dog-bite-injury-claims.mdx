Dog bites can cause deep puncture wounds, infections, nerve damage, scarring and lasting fear, and children are especially likely to be bitten on the face. If you or your child was bitten, you may have a claim against the dog's owner.

## How liability works

States take different approaches:

- **Strict liability.** Many states have statutes that make owners responsible for bites regardless of whether the dog had shown aggression before, with some exceptions such as trespassing or provoking the dog.
- **"One bite" rules.** Other states require showing the owner knew or should have known the dog was dangerous.
- **Negligence.** In many places you can also claim the owner was careless, for example by violating a leash law.

## Who actually pays

Most people don't want to sue a neighbor or friend. In practice, dog bite claims are usually paid by the owner's homeowners or renters insurance, not out of the owner's pocket.

## What to do after a dog bite

1. **Get medical care.** Dog bites have a high risk of infection, and rabies exposure must be evaluated.
2. **Identify the dog and owner.** Get names, address and vaccination information.
3. **Report the bite** to animal control or the local health department.
4. **Photograph your injuries** over time as they heal, and the place it happened.
5. **Get witness information.**
