A fully loaded tractor-trailer can weigh up to 80,000 pounds, about twenty times more than a typical car. When a truck crashes into a passenger vehicle, the injuries are often severe. Truck accident claims are also more complicated than ordinary car accident claims, which is why acting quickly matters.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Several companies may share responsibility, not just the driver.
- Federal rules govern driver hours, inspections and maintenance.
- Key evidence, like electronic logs, can be overwritten or lost if no one asks for it to be preserved.
- Trucking companies often have large insurance policies and rapid-response legal teams.

</KeyTakeaways>

## More parties, more insurance

In a car crash, the claim is usually against one driver and their insurer. In a truck crash, potentially responsible parties include:

- **The driver**, for speeding, distraction, fatigue or impairment
- **The motor carrier**, which may be responsible for its driver's conduct and for its own negligent hiring, training or supervision
- **The cargo loader**, if an overloaded or unsecured load caused the crash
- **The maintenance provider**, if brakes, tires or lights were not properly serviced
- **The manufacturer** of a defective part

Commercial carriers are required to carry higher liability insurance than ordinary drivers, and identifying all available coverage can make a significant difference.

## Federal safety rules

Interstate trucking is regulated by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA). Its rules limit how many hours a driver can be on duty, require drug and alcohol testing, set standards for vehicle inspection and maintenance, and require carriers to keep records. Violations of these rules can be strong evidence of negligence.

## Evidence disappears quickly

Trucking companies often send investigators to a crash scene within hours. Meanwhile, some records are only required to be kept for limited periods, and electronic data can be overwritten. A lawyer can send a preservation letter demanding that the carrier keep logs, dashcam footage, black box data and maintenance records.

## What to do after a truck crash

Follow the same basic steps as [any car accident](/guides/car-accidents/what-to-do-after-a-car-accident): get medical care, call the police, and document the scene. In addition, try to photograph the truck's company name, USDOT number (usually on the door of the cab), and trailer number. Do not give a statement to the trucking company's insurer before getting advice.
