Losing someone suddenly is devastating. When the death was caused by someone else's carelessness, such as a drunk driver, a negligent trucking company, or a medical error, the law may allow the family to hold them accountable through a wrongful death claim.

A wrongful death claim can pay for funeral costs and replace the income and support your loved one would have provided. It also holds the responsible party accountable.

## What a wrongful death claim is

A wrongful death claim is a civil lawsuit brought when a person dies because of another party's negligent, reckless or intentional conduct. It can be based on almost any situation that would have supported a personal injury claim if the person had survived.

## Who can bring a claim

Each state's law decides who may file and who can benefit. Commonly:

- A surviving spouse or domestic partner
- Children
- Parents, especially of a minor child
- In some states, other dependents or relatives

Many states require the claim to be filed by the personal representative (executor) of the estate on behalf of the family.

## What may be recovered

Depending on the state, damages may include:

- Medical bills from the final injury or illness
- Funeral and burial expenses
- Lost income and benefits the person would have provided
- Loss of companionship, guidance and care
- In some states, the pain and suffering the person experienced before death, through a related "survival" claim

## Deadlines

Wrongful death claims have filing deadlines that can differ from ordinary injury claims, and the clock often starts on the date of death. Claims against government entities may require formal notice much sooner. It is wise to talk to an attorney early, even if you are not ready to decide anything.
