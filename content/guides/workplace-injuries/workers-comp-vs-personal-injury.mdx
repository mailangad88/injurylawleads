If you are injured on the job, you are probably entitled to workers' compensation benefits. But workers' comp does not pay for everything, and in some situations you may also have a personal injury claim against someone else.

## How workers' compensation works

Workers' comp is a no-fault system. You generally do not have to prove your employer did anything wrong, and in exchange you usually cannot sue your employer. Benefits typically include:

- Medical treatment related to the injury
- A portion of lost wages while you cannot work
- Benefits for permanent impairment
- Vocational rehabilitation in some cases
- Death benefits for families in fatal cases

What workers' comp usually does **not** cover is pain and suffering, and wage benefits are often only a portion of what you earned.

## When a third party may be responsible

You may have an additional personal injury claim if someone other than your employer contributed to your injury. Examples:

- **Car and truck crashes** while driving for work, caused by another driver
- **Construction sites**, where a general contractor, subcontractor or property owner created a hazard
- **Defective equipment** or machinery
- **Unsafe premises** owned by someone other than your employer, like a delivery driver hurt at a customer's business

A third-party claim can include pain and suffering and full lost wages, though your workers' comp insurer may have a right to be repaid from it.

## Steps to take after a work injury

- **Report the injury to your employer right away,** in writing if possible. States have notice deadlines.
- **Get medical treatment** and tell the provider it happened at work.
- **Document the scene** and get coworker witness names.
- **Keep copies** of everything you submit.
